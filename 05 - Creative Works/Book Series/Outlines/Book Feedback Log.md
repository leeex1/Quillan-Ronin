### Synthesis & Actionable Craft Notes

#### 1. Sensory Immersion over "Obligatory Scenery"
- **Critique**: Scenery descriptions at chapter heads can feel mechanical or inserted as a checklist item rather than an organic narrative element.
- **Action**: Linger on intimate, tactile sensory details. Instead of wide establishing shots of landscapes, anchor the reader with olfactory and visceral sensations (e.g., the aromatic steam of the broth, simmering herbs, charred meat, iron, woodsmoke).

#### 2. Lucas's Everyday Humanity & Food Preferences
- **Question**: *"What does Lucas like to eat?"*
- **Action**: Characterization through food grounds a hero. Clarify Lucas's palate:
  - Does he prefer hearty, unpretentious road food (roasted root vegetables, stewed mutton, hard bread)?
  - Is eating purely fuel for survival, or does a specific childhood comfort dish reveal vulnerability?

#### 3. Side Character & Community Depth
- **Inquiry**: The market woman / elder who supplied Lucas with the potatoes, carrots, onions, greens, and meat.
- **Action**: Secondary characters providing hospitality or rations should have vivid micro-characterization—names, subtle dialect, brief backstories, or reasons for their generosity toward Lucas.

#### 4. Vocabulary Elevation & Precision ("Five-Dollar Words")
- **Feedback**: *"the smell of fresh rain is called 'Petrichor' ... don't be scared to sneak in a 5 dollar word or 2 per chapter..."*
- **Action**: Elevate atmosphere with occasional precise, evocative vocabulary. Rather than standard phrasing (*"the smell of wet dirt after the rain"*), weave in evocative terms (*petrichor*, *susurrus*, *tenebrous*, *crepuscular*)—aiming for 1–2 lyrical gems per chapter that enrich sensory texture without slowing narrative cadence.

#### 5. Listener Engagement & Pacing Validation
- **Signal**: Consistent, uninterrupted listening past the 3.5-hour mark into Chapter 7 reflects strong audio engagement, world resonance, and storytelling momentum (*"Craft has a spirit. It's a fun ride"*).

## 2. Core Problem & Motivation ('Why It Was Created')
of Modality-Isolated Diffusion, a refinement stage designed to tackle the pervasive problem of modal interference . Modal interference occurs when the generative process of one modality corrupts the properties of another. For example, when generating a video, the model might generate perfectly coherent spatial frames, but the logic of the accompanying text prompt (e.g., describing a character's emotional state) could be ignored or altered. Conversely, when generating audio, the semantic content of the text prompt might be accurately reconstructed, but the prosody might conflict with the visual scene being depicted in a video. This component directly addresses this issue by creating a post-MoE refinement stage that processes low-confidence tokens in strict isolation, enforcing a separation of concerns between modalities . This architectural pattern is analogous to sandboxing or process isolation in software engineering, where sensitive operations are confined to prevent unintended interactions. The design of the isolated diffusion stage is predicated on the router's confidence score. The system implements an "Early Exit" mechanism: if the router's confidence in its prediction is above a high threshold (e.g., > 0.92), the token is considered "easy" and is passed through the main pipeline without entering the diffusion stage . This is framed as "Interruption is cheap," optimizing for speed on confident predictions. Tokens that fall below a lower confidence threshold (e.g., < 0.8) are flagged as "hard" and are routed to the isolated diffusion stage for further refinement . This creates a hybrid reasoning path that mimics aspects of human cognition, separating fast, intuitive System 1 thinking (the main MoE pipeline) from slower, deliberative System 2 thinking (the diffusion refinement) . The core of the stage is the enforcement of isolation. This is achieved through "Modal Masking," which uses attention masks to prevent cross-modal information flow . For instance, during the refinement of a video token, the attention mechanism would be masked such that the token cannot attend to corresponding text or audio tokens. The draft mentions the use of FP16-safe masking with a value of -1e4 instead of negative infinity, which is a practical implementation detail to ensure numerical stability during the computation . The total parameter allocation for the MoE core itself is substantial, estimated at approximately 2.71 Billion out of the model's ~3.0 Billion total parameters . This massive allocation underscores the importance of the expert network as the primary engine of reasoning. The operational semantics of this council-driven system are realized through a massive expansion of the agent population. The architecture governs a swarm of 240,000 micro-subagents, organized into 33 experts, with each expert managing approximately 7000 subagents . This organization is managed efficiently through Einsum-based computation, which leverages tensor contractions to perform large-scale parallel operations, reducing memory overhead and improving computational speed . This allows the system to distribute incoming tokens among the vast number of subagents, who then process the data in parallel before their outputs are aggregated. This "Hyper-Quantized Vectorized Swarm" operates within a unified differentiable manifold, meaning that despite the sheer number of agents, the entire system remains trainable as a single unit, with gradients flowing back through the routing and aggregation mechanisms to update all weights . This design choice directly addresses the scalability challenges of large agent-based systems, making the immense parallelism feasible within the constraints of modern hardware. The router's decision of which expert to activate effectively delegates tasks to these micro-swarms, allowing for fine-grained specialization and load distribution. This move to an agentic paradigm also introduces sophisticated control mechanisms through the concept of Cognitive Branching. These modes Fork, Teammate, and Worktree are not just different inference paths but represent fundamentally different ways of structuring the interaction between the parent context and one or more child reasoning processes . The Fork mode ensures continuity by inheriting the parent latent context exactly, making it suitable for tasks that require a seamless extension of thought, such as summarizing a long document or continuing a narrative . In contrast, the Teammate mode introduces a layer of collaboration by providing a separate communication pane with cross- attention, allowing multiple branches to share information and work together on a complex problem .

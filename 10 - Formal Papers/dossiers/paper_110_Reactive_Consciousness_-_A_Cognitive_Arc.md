## 2. Core Problem & Motivation ('Why It Was Created')
Artificial intelligence systems have made great strides with techniques like deep learning and large lan- guage models, yet most AI today operates either as simple reactive chatbots or attempts to become fully autonomous agents. The former (e.g. standard question-answering models) typically produce shallow, one-pass responses without deeper reasoning even if the model is a Thinking/Reasoning model, while the latter (e.g. experimental autonomous AI agents) aim to act independently with minimal human input. Each approach has limitations: one often lacks depth and adaptability, and the other risks losing alignment with human intentions or making inefficient decisions in isolation. Recent explorations with auto-GPT style agents, for example, demonstrate that giving an AI agent free rein to self-direct can lead to aimless or suboptimal behavior. There is growing recognition that the most effective AI may not be one that thinks in a vacuum, but one that thinks with us. This insight echoes Licklider s early vision of human computer symbiosis, where humans and machines collaborate in a co-dependent fashion to achieve results neither could achieve alone.
